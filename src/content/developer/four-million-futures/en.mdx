For the past couple of days, I've been playing this absurdly addictive footy game (rugby) with friends nonstop. My personal best is 95 in solo mode, and my wife hit a whopping 99. (Fun secret: the whole point is to set the volume you like, so there's technically no right answer.)

Anyway, I kept replaying it, and in puppy mode I could never get past 41. (You can shove the ball clean off the screen and still only max out at 41, lol.)

Then, while talking it over with my wife, we wondered: is a score above 40 even possible? Maybe the owner's calling time needs to be a second shorter? I was pretty convinced that was it. But wait, we can't actually know until we try every possible shot, right?

Is that even realistic? Wouldn't there be way too many? Actually, how many is "too many"?

The variables are where the kick starts and where you release after dragging. So it's roughly start points A × end points B... that's not that many, is it?

## What I Learned

- **Solo mode:** As expected, a run that displays 100 on screen is possible. To be exact, 99.954458.
- **Puppy mode:** Lots of the top scores come from physics glitches, but you can still hit around 90 legitimately by bouncing the ball into the air.

## Watch the Replays Yourself

**[→ Open Shot Lab: Solo / Puppy Top 10 Replays](/lab/)**

Hit `Load Solo Top 10` or `Load Puppy Top 10` to load my results and watch the replays right away. To run your own search, pick a mode and an interval, then press `Find high scores`.

Saved replays play back the exact frames of the ball and the puppy. Nothing is recomputed, so you see the same run I did. A new search, though, depends on more than the seed: input coordinates (which vary with screen size), exact shot values, and the initial state all affect it. So the same seed won't guarantee identical results across environments.
