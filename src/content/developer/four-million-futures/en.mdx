Since the day before yesterday, I've been playing this absurdly addictive rugby game (footy, if you're in Australia) with friends like a man possessed. My personal best is 95 (solo mode), and my wife hit a whopping 99. (It's a "set the volume you want" kind of game, so technically there's no right answer. Shh, don't tell anyone.)

Anyway, it's so addictive that I kept replaying it, and no matter what I did in puppy mode, the best I could get was 41. (Okay, that's actually just from smashing the ball clean off the screen. lol)

Then I was talking with my wife and wondered: is 40+ even possible in the first place? Maybe the time before the owner calls the puppy should be cut by a second or so. I convinced myself that was probably right. But wait... can we actually know that without running every possible shot?

Really? Isn't that way too many? But "too many" is how many, exactly?

The possibilities are the point where you start the kick, and then where you release after dragging. So roughly start points A times end points B, right? That's not a lot?

Me: Codex, doable?  
Codex: yep  
Me: Go.

## What I Learned

- Solo mode: Just as I suspected, a play that shows 100 on screen is possible. Precisely, it's 99.954458.
- Puppy mode: The search turned up a lot of high scores that abuse the physics glitch, but even without it, you can bounce the ball into the air for roughly 90.

## Watch the Replays Yourself

**[→ Open Shot Lab: Solo / Puppy Top 10 Replays](/lab/)**

Hit `Load Solo Top 10` or `Load Puppy Top 10` and you'll instantly load the results from my run and can watch the replays. If you want to search on your own, pick a mode and an interval, then hit `Find high scores`.

Saved replays play back the exact frames of the ball and puppy. Nothing is recalculated, so you're watching the very same run. A fresh search, though, depends on more than the seed: input coordinates that vary with screen size, the exact shot values, and the initial state all matter. So even with the same seed, I can't guarantee identical search results across environments.
