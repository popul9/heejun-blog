title: "I Can't Seem to Play Games."
description: "Is it a developer's occupational hazard, or is it just me? Besides games, there's way too much else to get lost in."

heroImage: "./nintendo-switch.webp"
---

Whenever I'm developing, it feels like I'm playing The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild.
Developing feels like gaming, and gaming feels like developing.

What's over that mountain? Oh, a shrine, let me check it out. Then I wander off again. Wait, what was I trying to do in the first place?

I'm driving and suddenly: wait... can this actually be built? So I ask AI right away and try whatever I need to try, right then and there. Once I get the basic principle, the implementation is a piece of cake.
Oh, if this works, then that should work too, right? I try that like a madman, too.

Then somewhere in the middle of all that frantic building: wait, where am I even going? Okay, it works, but is anyone besides me ever going to use this? Did I overcomplicate it?
Did I just snap out of it to find I've built something that reflects nothing but my own extreme taste?

Then I run into a syntax I've never seen before. Oh, neat. So in .NET you can write code like this, too?
Hmm... but this makes the code less simple. Is there a clear advantage over the old syntax?

Then, working in a daze like any other day, it suddenly hits me: is this even worth anything? Sure, it's fine as long as I'm having fun, but can I really be someone who runs on the joy of technology alone? Does my effort actually pay off in the market? So what does that make me, a developer who only chases things that don't pay?

Dig into UX and another thought pops up. If it's going to be like this, why not just make a game? The player should feel like they're in control, but I can't pile on so much that it overwhelms them. Apparently you can do C# development with Unity on a Mac, so maybe I'll try it as a test? For now, I jot it down in my TODO notes.
Can't bring myself to play games, yet I want to make one.

Apparently AI is getting into philosophy now? Not just training it on "answers" anymore, but training it on the logical "flow" of my thinking, separately?? So should I go learn baking, to play it safe? But then baking is just a matter of time too, and machines will replace everything eventually, right?

Hmm...
Looks like I'm in deep, in the Hyrule that is IT. There's way too much to get lost in besides games.
