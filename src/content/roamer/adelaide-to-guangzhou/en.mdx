## Adelaide to Guangzhou

We left Adelaide and headed to Guangzhou first.
A direct flight makes things so easy. It was a Thursday morning, and Guangzhou was the only international departure at the time. 

Thankfully, China Southern flies direct to Guangzhou. I took this flight when I first came to Australia too.

![The safety video on the China Southern inflight screen before departure](./china-southern-safety-video.avif)


## Finally watching The Battle at Lake Changjin

As soon as I boarded, I watched The Battle at Lake Changjin, the huge Chinese hit about the Korean War.

![The Chinese film The Battle at Lake Changjin on the inflight screen](./battle-at-lake-changjin-screen.avif)

I was curious about how China sees the Korean War.


What struck me was that it's the Korean War, set entirely in Korea, yet there are no Koreans. Just Chinese and American soldiers. 

I got curious and looked it up. China had been preparing to land troops in Taiwan, but that plan became uncertain when the US Seventh Fleet was deployed to the Taiwan Strait on 27 June 1950 (after North Korea triggered the war). As far as I know, China barely had a navy then. 
I also learned how China, seeing a clash with the US as increasingly unavoidable, came to intervene on the Korean Peninsula as American forces approached its border during the war. I'd learned about the Korean War in fairly simple terms. This gave me a fuller picture.

It hurt to think that so many people died arguing over which ideology was right, when those ideologies aren't even tangible things.
