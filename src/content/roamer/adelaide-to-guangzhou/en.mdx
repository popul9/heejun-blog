## Adelaide to Guangzhou

We left Adelaide and headed to Guangzhou first.
The direct flight makes it wonderfully easy. It was a Thursday morning, and our Guangzhou flight was literally the only international departure on the board... lol. Flights from Adelaide often go through Sydney or Melbourne, so on a day like this you really feel just how small the airport is.

When I first moved to Adelaide, I took this same China Southern direct flight from Guangzhou in the other direction. That was all the way back in 2017, nine years ago now. The route disappeared for a while during COVID, came back seasonally, and apparently returned to year-round service in 2026. It felt a little strange to be taking the plane that once brought me here, only backwards this time, heading into China.

![The pre-departure safety video on the China Southern inflight screen](./china-southern-safety-video.avif)

That exciting little pre-departure safety video?
I don't think it has changed since nine years ago. Weirdly comforting, haha.

## Meeting China before we even landed

As soon as I boarded, I watched *The Battle at Lake Changjin*, China's massive hit about the Korean War.

![The Chinese film The Battle at Lake Changjin playing on the inflight screen](./battle-at-lake-changjin-screen.avif)

I had wanted to see it for ages, so this was a nice chance. I was curious about the Chinese perspective on the Korean War. As far as I know, it became the country's highest-grossing film at the time and was a huge commercial success.

As a Korean, one thing felt particularly unusual: the film starts with the American landing at Incheon, and all the terrain and locations are Korean, but there is no Korean language and you barely see either the North or South Korean armies. It is presented almost entirely as a battle between China and the United States. I'm not sure there's even a right answer to which ideology was right or wrong, but it was painful to watch so many people kill one another.

I got curious and read more about the background afterwards. China had been preparing an invasion of Taiwan, but that plan was delayed when the US Seventh Fleet entered the Taiwan Strait on 27 June 1950. As the war later expanded closer to the Chinese border, that also became part of the context for China's intervention on the Korean Peninsula. Seeing another perspective on the same war was fascinating. Growing up and being educated in Korea, I had understood it rather simply as China entering because it did not want the United States coming too close. The wider context helped me understand why China made the decision despite knowing it would bring so many casualties.

## Arriving in Guangzhou

Anyway, two full movies made the flight pass much faster than I expected, and before long we had arrived. :)

And what greets you when you land? A Xiaomi car, apparently... haha.

![A Xiaomi car advertisement at Guangzhou airport](./guangzhou-airport-xiaomi-ad.avif)

We were staying one night in Guangzhou, so we took the metro to our accommodation. It felt so much like the Korean subway that for a moment I thought I had somehow landed in Seoul... haha.

![Inside a Guangzhou Metro carriage](./guangzhou-metro-carriage.avif)

Inside the carriage, even the seat layout and handrails felt almost identical to the Korean subway.
