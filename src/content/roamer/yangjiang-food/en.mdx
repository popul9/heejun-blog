At the first restaurant my wife's brother took us to, we ordered cheung fun and rice noodles. The bowls came with different toppings: prawn, beef and pork.

This stuff is genuinely *so* good. Everything tastes quite gentle, yet the rice noodles are impossibly thin and soft and almost melt in your mouth. It tastes magically delicious. I honestly do not understand how a combination of such mild flavours can be this good 😅

You can find cheung fun almost anywhere in China, but every region uses a slightly different combination of fillings, so each one has its own character. Maybe think of the way Korean gimbap or sundae changes a little from region to region. The cheung fun here had bean sprouts inside.

## Dinner in Yangjiang: the goose restaurant

![The entrance to a restaurant specialising in goose on the edge of Yangjiang](./goose-restaurant-entrance.avif)

A little outside the city centre is a goose restaurant with a serious reputation. I had been here before, but it has become much more famous since then. Apparently even visitors from out of town now make a point of coming here when they are in Yangjiang.

![The busy dining room packed with customers](./goose-restaurant-dining-room.avif)

This is roughly what it looks like inside. If you spot an empty table, just move quickly and claim it! haha.

![Teacups and tableware stacked on an empty table](./goose-restaurant-tableware.avif)

![Rinsing the tableware with the first pour of tea and a pair of tongs](./rinsing-tableware-with-tea.avif)

Once you have a seat, they set the table at speed. Naturally, tea arrives too, and the first pour is used to rinse the tableware.

The wings and feet came out first. The texture is incredibly chewy. They are coated in a soy-based sauce and topped with what looks like ground peanuts, so they are nutty, a little sweet, and very satisfying to nibble away at.

### Goose offal and blood pudding

![Goose offal stir-fried with spring onion](./stir-fried-goose-intestines.avif)

![Goose blood pudding stir-fried with green chillies](./stir-fried-blood-pudding.avif)

Next came goose offal and blood pudding. It is hard to be completely certain about the exact cuts from the photos alone, but judging by its shape and texture, the first plate looks like intestine or a similar kind of offal. The second is set goose blood stir-fried with green chillies. The spring onion and green chilli worked really well with both.

### Stir-fried tofu and pork

![Tofu and pork stir-fried together](./tofu-and-pork-stir-fry.avif)

This one is tofu stir-fried with pork, and it was ridiculously tasty too. The rich savouriness of the pork fat lifts everything, while the plain softness of the tofu makes it feel warm and easy. Proper comfort food.

### Goose fried rice

![Fried rice with finely sliced goose and spring onion](./goose-fried-rice.avif)

And now, the main event: goose fried rice! First of all, the fried rice itself is just excellent. The rice is a little longer than what is commonly eaten in Korea or Japan, so when it is fried, every grain gets beautifully coated in oil. Thin slices of goose fit it perfectly. It is the sort of thing I could eat often without getting tired of it.

## Breakfast in Yangjiang: dim sum

![The entrance to the dim sum restaurant we visited the next morning](./dim-sum-restaurant-exterior.avif)

The next day, we headed straight to a dim sum restaurant.

![An open kitchen where several chefs are busily making dim sum](./dim-sum-kitchen.avif)

As you walk in, you can see several cooks working away, almost like a scene from *Spirited Away*.

![A huge hall full of people enjoying breakfast and tea](./dim-sum-dining-hall.avif)

Further inside was a huge dining hall where everyone was enjoying breakfast-slash-brunch. People here have a tradition of gathering with the extended family, drinking tea, slowly tasting dim sum one piece at a time and talking. They used to smoke at the table as well, but indoor smoking is banned now. I do not smoke, yet somehow it feels as if one little piece of the tradition has disappeared 😅

![A restaurant menu showing the many kinds of dim sum in photographs](./dim-sum-menu.avif)

The menu is enormous. Thankfully every dish has a photo, which makes ordering wonderfully easy.

![Rinsing the tableware with water from the teapot](./rinsing-dim-sum-tableware-1.avif)

![Holding a teacup with tongs and rinsing it in hot water](./rinsing-dim-sum-tableware-2.avif)

My wife's brother expertly rinsed everything with tea. At a Korean barbecue restaurant, someone always has to keep the tongs moving and cook the meat. Here, the busy person is the one in charge of the teapot 😅 In Korea you hold tongs to grill meat; here you hold little tongs to wash the cups.

### Dim sum and seafood soup

As you can probably tell from the photos, everything was delicious. The ingredients were fresh, and sipping tea between dishes reset my palate, so I could keep noticing the particular flavour of each piece of dim sum.

![A breakfast table beginning to fill with steamed buns, dim sum and tea](./dim-sum-table-spread.avif)

The table slowly filling up with steamed buns, dim sum and tea.

![Moist siu mai topped with pieces of prawn](./shrimp-siu-mai.avif)

Siu mai topped with prawn, moist and bouncy.

![Dark green dim sum steamed on a leaf](./steamed-dim-sum.avif)

Dark green dim sum steamed on a leaf. The aroma was distinctive.

![Dim sum wrapped in red rice sheets with blanched vegetables](./red-rice-rolls.avif)

Dim sum wrapped in red rice sheets, served with blanched vegetables.

![A warm soup with vegetables and seafood](./seafood-soup.avif)

We also had a soup that felt a little like Korean sujebi. It had bits of seafood, meat and all sorts of other ingredients, making it rich but still gentle and easy to eat.

![Lifting a thin, soft piece of dough from the seafood soup](./seafood-soup-close-up.avif)

Thin, soft pieces of dough hiding in the soup.

More than anything, this dim sum meal struck me as rather wise. I love food and can overeat very easily, but here there is always a tiny teacup sitting beside me. Whenever I start getting greedy, the cup seems to remind me:

> Don't be greedy with food. Slow down, taste it properly and enjoy this moment. There is no reason to rush to eat. :)

![Soft steamed peanuts that you peel before eating](./boiled-peanuts.avif)

Oh, and these steamed peanuts felt a little unusual too. Peeling them is fun, and unlike the roasted peanuts I usually eat, their soft, moist texture is oddly charming.

This post has become far too long, so I am saving the rest of the food for part two. We ate way too many good things... 😅
