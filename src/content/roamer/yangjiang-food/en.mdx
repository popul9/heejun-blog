## First meal

![The neighbourhood restaurant we visited for our first meal in Yangjiang](./first-noodle-shop.avif)
Came here on my wife's brother's recommendation. Nothing particularly special about the place at first glance. 

![Our first meal, with prawn, beef and pork rice noodles and cheung fun](./noodles-and-cheung-fun.avif)
Hang on. The flavour is so gentle, but the rice noodles are impossibly thin and melt in your mouth. Can texture alone make something this good? 

![A cross section of Yangjiang cheung fun filled with bean sprouts](./bean-sprout-cheung-fun.avif)
A filling with a bit of local character. Dip it in the spicy sauce and the savouriness goes through the roof.

## One whole goose

![The entrance to a goose restaurant on the outskirts of Yangjiang](./goose-restaurant-entrance.avif)

This is the famous goose place. Locals + visitors all have to try it. There's nothing else around, but so many people come for this restaurant that street vendors have set up out the front too.

![The goose restaurant full of diners](./goose-restaurant-dining-room.avif)

This is what it's like inside. Spot an empty table, move fast and claim it.

Once you sit down, they set the table in no time. Tea comes out here too, of course, and the first pour goes towards rinsing the dishes.

![Goose feet with soy sauce and ground peanuts](./goose-wings-and-feet-1.avif)

![Chewy pieces of goose wing on a plate](./goose-wings-and-feet-2.avif)

Goose ought to be a bit different, right? First up, these enormous feet and wings greet you. Just making sure you remember what you came here to eat. The recipe goes: 

> Soy sauce + ground peanuts + chewiness = the perfect appetiser.


![Goose offal stir fried with spring onion](./stir-fried-goose-intestines.avif)

![Goose blood pudding stir fried with green chillies](./stir-fried-blood-pudding.avif)

The rest of the goose. We've already established that it tastes good. Different parts, different pleasures.

![Tofu stir fried with pork](./tofu-and-pork-stir-fry.avif)

Tofu stir fried with pork, taking everything that's good about pork fat to its peak. Comforting, happy food.

### Today's main event

![Fried rice with finely chopped goose and spring onion](./goose-fried-rice.avif)

The kind of flavour I could eat often without getting bored. Here too, the star arrives last.

## What about dim sum?

![The entrance to the dim sum restaurant we visited the next morning in Yangjiang](./dim-sum-restaurant-exterior.avif)

Straight to a dim sum place. Apparently this huge restaurant opened recently. 

![Cooks busy making dim sum in the open kitchen](./dim-sum-kitchen.avif)

Walk in and the cooks are bustling about like something out of Spirited Away.

![A huge dining hall full of people having breakfast and tea](./dim-sum-dining-hall.avif)

People enjoying dim sum in a massive hall. There used to be a smell of cigarette smoke too (that was just part of the culture), but there's less of it these days. I don't even smoke, yet somehow I miss it a little.

There's an absolutely gigantic TV on the wall. Leaving the TV on when the family gets together seems to be universal.

![A restaurant menu showing photos of the dim sum dishes](./dim-sum-menu.avif)

Glad there are photos. Choosing what to eat, though, requires the same care as filling in the bubbles on an exam answer sheet.

![Rinsing the dishes with water from the teapot](./rinsing-dim-sum-tableware-1.avif)

![Holding a teacup with small tongs to rinse it in hot water](./rinsing-dim-sum-tableware-2.avif)

My wife's brother expertly rinses the dishes with tea first. At a barbecue, the person holding the meat tongs is the busy one. Here, it's whoever sits next to the teapot. 

Small tongs instead of barbecue tongs, rinsing dishes and filling cups. So grateful.

### Here comes the dim sum.

![Steamed buns, dim sum and tea arriving at the breakfast table](./dim-sum-table-spread.avif)

Buns came first. 

![Moist siu mai topped with prawn](./shrimp-siu-mai.avif)

Prawn siu mai. Packed full.

![Dark green dim sum steamed on a leaf](./steamed-dim-sum.avif)

Mugwort dim sum steamed on a leaf.

![Red rice rolls and blanched greens](./red-rice-rolls.avif)

Dim sum wrapped in red rice sheets. Fried breadcrumbs inside, so it's crunchy but chewy. First time you eat it, you wonder what sort of fried ice cream combination this is. It's so popular that every restaurant serves it. 

![Warm soup with vegetables and seafood](./seafood-soup.avif)

There was a soup that reminded me of Korean sujebi too. Little bits of seafood, different meats and all sorts of ingredients. Full of flavour, but easy to enjoy.

![A thin, soft piece of dough lifted from the seafood soup](./seafood-soup-close-up.avif)

Thin, soft pieces of dough in the soup. Like I said, there's some kind of magic in the flour here. The texture alone has already won me over.

Oh, and a bit of wisdom I can't leave out: the tea culture. There's always a little teacup beside me (and someone kindly keeping it topped up 🤭)

The cup reminds me.

> Don't just chase the food. Enjoy this moment. Savour it slowly. There's no reason to rush a meal.

![Soft steamed peanuts eaten straight from their shells](./boiled-peanuts.avif)

The steamed peanuts are part of this rhythm too. As you shell them, fullness slowly catches up with you. Ah, that was good.

Again, have you seen Chihiro eating those rice balls Haku put a spell on in Spirited Away? Looking like they're the best thing ever? Anyway, that's how I ate. Even plain rice would probably taste amazing here.
