So fresh you can tell at a glance.

Those colours. A work of art.

They check the kernels inside before you buy the corn.

Pre-cut vegetables. Bought a little, just enough for that day. Cheap too.

## Meat

![A small stall inside the market](./img_5742.avif)

Geese and chickens.

![Meat laid out at a stall](./img_5743.avif)

![Hanging meat and a scale](./img_5746.avif)

The meat is usually butchered and sold on the same day, so they hang it at room temperature. Easy to see what's on offer, that's for sure. But leaving it out like this, so boldly? Here's why, I think:

1. It's incredibly fresh. I didn't see flies on it once. In this heat, customers would know straight away if the meat were getting old.
2. People buy just enough to eat that day. Buying loads and putting it in the fridge or freezer doesn't seem to be the thing here.
3. Apparently there's a separate market for meat that doesn't sell that day, where nearby restaurants and others buy it in bulk.

Maybe this is why the food in Yangjiang tastes so good. In hot places, people often salt or season food to keep it longer. Here, by the sea, with plenty of food coming in, people expect fresh ingredients all the time.

![Long strips of pork](./img_5861.avif)

Look at those layers of fat and lean meat. That colour alone looks bloody good..

<Video src="/videos/yangjiang-market-img-5873.mp4" />

Ordered a little pork rib. The butcher chopped it up, bone and all, in no time.

![A meat dish and a bowl of rice porridge on the table](./img_5908.avif)

This is ridiculous. Steam the ribs with fermented black beans, eat them with rice porridge, and the taste just about knocks you out. Had it three times while we were there.

## Chicken

![Ingredients being prepared](./img_5863.avif)

I didn't know yolks could form inside a hen before the eggs do. Cooked up, they're tasty too.

![A dish of vegetables and meat](./img_6093.avif)

![A piece of meat held up with chopsticks](./img_6095.avif)

And this is what it becomes. One problem: since coming home, every time I eat chicken I think of this and get a bit sad. Like I said, the texture and the flavour of the meat are different.

## Seafood

![A market aisle and fish stalls](./img_6058.avif)

![A stall selling fish and seafood](./img_5858.avif)

The fish is sold fresh too.

## Prepared-food shop

![Roast meat behind a glass display](./img_5876.avif)

Last stop, a shop selling ready-made dishes. Anything that's hard to make at home, we buy here. Who said a meal at home has to be plain? We ate better than that.

## And then, dinner at home

![A small bowl of soup](./img_6033.avif)

The rule before every meal: start with soup made that day. Cooked that day, finished that day.

![A table set with several dishes](./img_6032.avif)

![A bite of food picked up with chopsticks](./img_5895.avif)

Candied Chinese yam (拔丝山药) from the ready-made food shop. Basi? Loved it with my very childish sweet tooth.

![A table full of food](./img_6084.avif)

Another slightly terrifying feast. My wife's brother cooked for us. Seafood festival. First time trying mantis shrimp.

## Other things

![A long loofah](./img_5736.avif)

The loofah (a natural bath sponge to me) was huge. I wanted one. No idea why, but having one at home feels like it'd be reassuring.

## What about at night?

![A food stall lit up at night](./img_5778.avif)

Thought the market would quiet down at night. Nope. Street stalls come out and it's packed all over again. Food straight from the wok. How could it not be good? I was jogging, so I only went past.

![A Welsh corgi by the chairs outside a shop](./img_5775.avif)

But even at night it's really hot. The corgi agrees.
