## Guangzhou South railway station

The next morning, we called a DiDi and headed to Guangzhou South to catch the high-speed train to Yangjiang. DiDi is basically China's Uber: call a car in the app and the driver takes you where you need to go.

![On the way to Guangzhou South in a DiDi on a rainy morning](./didi-to-guangzhou-south-station.avif)

The terminal is enormous. It already looked big from outside, but once we walked in, the waiting hall seemed to go on forever under that high ceiling. There were several high-speed trains lined up side by side at the platforms too.

## On to Yangjiang

The ride from Guangzhou to Yangjiang takes about two hours. It was the same last time I came, but the view outside is just *so* green the whole way. There is plenty of water and the weather is good, so we passed a lot of farms too. For an outsider like me, though, it is so humid that merely breathing seems to make me sweat... haha.

![Rainy green countryside in Guangdong seen through the high-speed train window](./guangdong-countryside-from-train.avif)

![Yangjiang railway station and its surroundings beneath a clear blue sky](./yangjiang-railway-station.avif)

And then we arrived in sunny Yangjiang! :) It had been raining in Guangzhou, but the moment we stepped off in Yangjiang the sky was blue and the weather was ridiculously nice.

## Our first ride in Yangjiang

My wife's brother came to pick us up. He had an electric car made by a company called AION, and it was incredibly roomy and comfortable inside. The screen was huge and showed nearby cars and people as it detected them in real time. It was my first ride in an EV and... wow, they really are nice. Apparently the price is pretty reasonable too 😲 He said most people buying a new car these days go electric.

![The white AION electric SUV that came to pick us up outside Yangjiang station](./aion-electric-suv.avif)

![The spacious interior of the AION EV with its large centre screen](./aion-electric-car-interior.avif)

Then we went straight out for our first meal of the day. It was the sort of easy neighbourhood place you can wander into for a quick meal, roughly the Chinese equivalent of a Korean Gimbap Cheonguk.

![Inside the neighbourhood restaurant where we had our first meal in Yangjiang](./yangjiang-noodle-shop.avif)

![Three bowls of noodles and a plate of cheung fun at our first Yangjiang meal](./yangjiang-noodles-and-cheung-fun.avif)

This place serves absolutely incredible noodles and cheung fun, but I'll save the proper food talk for the next post :)
